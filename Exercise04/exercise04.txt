Exercise 4: time vs number of processors, and speedup
Run on: Debian Linux container (Docker), Open MPI 5.0.7, 8 cpus. Times are the median of 5 runs.
Plots: python plot.py (needs matplotlib) -> sum_mpi.png (Exercise 2), montecarlo_mpi.png (Exercise 3)
(The sheet says "Exercise 1 and Exercise 2", but Exercise 1 is the repo setup;
the two programs to measure are Exercises 2 and 3.)

Exercise 2: sum of 1 to 10,000,000
processes   time (s)    speedup
1           0.003256    1.00
2           0.001656    1.97
4           0.000930    3.50
8           0.000925    3.52

Exercise 3: Monte Carlo Pi
processes   time (s)    speedup
1           0.133954    1.00
2           0.067256    1.99
4           0.034630    3.87
8           0.024338    5.50

Both programs scale almost perfectly up to 4 processes. With 8 they stop improving:
- Exercise 2 is very small work (3 ms in total), so with 8 processes the time is mostly
  starting up and sending the 7 partial sums, and the time stays at about 0.9 ms.
- Exercise 3 has more work per point (random numbers), so it keeps improving to 5.5x,
  but not 8x: this machine has 8 cpus, but 4 of them are slower efficiency cores, and
  the slowest process decides when rank 0 gets the last result.
